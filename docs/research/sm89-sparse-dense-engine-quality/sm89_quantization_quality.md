# Quantization and Weight Formats for Quality on sm_89 (Ada), BF16 Reference

## What quantization formats are available and their quality (IQ4_XS, Q4_K, Q5_K, Q6_K, Q8_0, fp8, int8, NVFP4)?

### Takeaway
For GGUF/ggml-style local inference, K-quants and i-quants (notably `Q4_K_M`, `Q5_K_M`, `Q6_K`, `IQ4_XS`) are the dominant low-bit weight formats, with quality usually measured against FP16/BF16 by perplexity delta and KLD; `Q8_0` is commonly treated as near-lossless, while 4-bit quality depends strongly on calibration (imatrix), tensor mix, and model family. On Ada/sm_89-class datacenter/GPU serving stacks, `W8A8-FP8` is a first-class path (vLLM/llm-compressor, LMDeploy SmoothQuant/FP8 paths), INT8/AWQ INT4 are widely supported, and NVFP4/MXFP4 are newer 4-bit floating formats whose practical support/quality targets newer kernels/hardware generations (with Blackwell-oriented support emphasized in recent tooling docs). — [llama.cpp quantize README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/quantize/README.md); [llama.cpp imatrix README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/imatrix/README.md); [VisionPsy-Nano-460M-GGUFs](https://huggingface.co/qvac/VisionPsy-Nano-460M-GGUFs); [LMDeploy W4A16](https://github.com/InternLM/lmdeploy/blob/main/docs/en/quantization/w4a16.md), [W8A8](https://github.com/InternLM/lmdeploy/blob/main/docs/en/quantization/w8a8.md); [llm-compressor quantization schemes](https://github.com/vllm-project/llm-compressor/blob/main/docs/steps/choosing-scheme.md).

### Cited Findings
- `llama-quantize` supports a broad set of GGUF quantization types and explicitly recommends imatrix-based optimization to reduce quality loss; it also exposes advanced per-tensor control (`--tensor-type`), output/embedding overrides (`--output-tensor-type`, `--token-embedding-type`), and `--pure` to disable k-quant mixtures. — [llama.cpp tools/quantize/README.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/tools/quantize/README.md)
- llama.cpp documents legacy and K-quant families with bits/weight and tradeoffs (e.g. `Q2_K..Q6_K`, `Q8_0`, F16) and shows performance/size tables; K-quants use superblock structures (e.g. `Q4_K`/`Q5_K` ~4.5/5.5 bpw in tensor encoding schemes; `Q6_K` 6.5625 bpw). — [llama.cpp quantize README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/tools/quantize/README.md); [ggml-org/llama.cpp tensor encoding schemes (via Context7 summary)](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/wiki/Tensor-Encoding-Schemes)
- i-quants in llama.cpp/gguf include `IQ4_XS` (4.25 bpw class in common usage), `IQ4_NL`, `IQ3_*`, `IQ2_*`, etc.; imatrix is especially associated with quality-sensitive low-bit/i-quant and mixed recipes. — [llama.cpp imatrix README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/tools/imatrix/README.md); [VisionPsy-Nano-460M-GGUFs](https://huggingface.co/qvac/VisionPsy-Nano-460M-GGUFs)
- A concrete HF example (VisionPsy-Nano-460M, 460M VLM, GGUFs generated with llama.cpp, imatrix for sub-8-bit variants except legacy Q4_0) reports normalized benchmark deltas vs FP32 baseline: `Q8_0` ~lossless (≈0.00), `Q5_K_M` −0.12 pts (−0.19%), `Q4_K_M` (imatrix) −0.24 pts (−0.39%), `IQ4_XS` (imatrix) −0.92 pts (−1.49%), `IQ4_NL` (imatrix) −0.96 pts (−1.55%), legacy `Q4_0` −2.85 pts (−4.62%). Vision encoder/mmproj fixed to Q8_0 in that release. — [VisionPsy-Nano-460M-GGUFs model card](https://huggingface.co/qvac/VisionPsy-Nano-460M-GGUFs)
- Community/HF model cards frequently report `llama-perplexity --kl-divergence` (KLD) and PPL against BF16/F16 for GGUF quants (e.g. IQ4_XS/imat vs other 4-bit recipes), indicating KLD+PPL as common quality gates in practice; exact numbers are model/dataset/calibration-dependent. — [6block/Hy4-preview-GGUF](https://huggingface.co/6block/Hy4-preview-GGUF); [webmp3/Sakura-Fara1.5-4B-GGUF](https://huggingface.co/webmp3/Sakura-Fara1.5-4B-GGUF); [na0x2c6/Clover-35B-A3B-Preview-GGUF](https://huggingface.co/na0x2c6/Clover-35B-A3B-Preview-GGUF)
- LMDeploy documents AWQ/GPTQ INT4 (W4A16-style grouped quantization) and W8A8 (INT8 and FP8) support; Ada Lovelace (sm89, 40 series) is listed as supported for AWQ/GPTQ INT4 inference and for INT8/FP8 inference paths in the cited docs. — [LMDeploy W4A16 docs](https://github.com/InternLM/lmdeploy/blob/main/docs/en/quantization/w4a16.md); [LMDeploy W8A8/SmoothQuant docs](https://github.com/InternLM/lmdeploy/blob/main/docs/en/quantization/w8a8.md)
- llm-compressor documents scheme/hardware targeting: `W8A8-FP8` targets Lovelace+ (min compute capability 8.9) for high throughput on modern GPUs; `NVFP4`/`MXFP4`/`MXFP8` are listed as Blackwell (SM100, min compute capability 10.0) oriented in that table, while `W4A16/W8A16` and `W8A8-INT8` target older capability floors. — [llm-compressor Choosing the right compression scheme](https://github.com/vllm-project/llm-compressor/blob/main/docs/steps/choosing-scheme.md)
- vLLM/llm-compressor ecosystem supports multiple quantization/compression paths (FP8, INT8, INT4 variants, NVFP4/MXFP4-related tooling evolving) for deployment; quality retention is workload- and recipe-dependent and typically validated by task evals/perplexity in model cards. — [vLLM quantization docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/quantization/); [llm-compressor README/docs](https://github.com/vllm-project/llm-compressor)

### Inferences
- For sm_89/Ada quality-first work, `Q8_0` (GGUF) and FP8 weight/activation or BF16 baselines are the safest high-fidelity references; 4–6 bit GGUF (`Q6_K`, `Q5_K_M`, `Q4_K_M`, `IQ4_XS`+imatrix) can be close but must be validated per-model with KLD/PPL/task suites rather than assumed equivalent to BF16.
- `IQ4_XS` can retain strong quality in some releases when imatrix-calibrated (e.g. VisionPsy-Nano: −1.49% normalized vs FP32 baseline), but it is not universally “BF16-equivalent” and legacy non-imatrix 4-bit baselines (e.g. Q4_0) can degrade much more.
- NVFP4/MXFP4 are relevant to forward-looking artifact/kernels but, per current public scheme tables, should not be assumed as primary quality-preserving paths on sm_89 without engine-specific validation.

### Gaps
- No single cross-model, sm_89-specific leaderboard was found that directly ranks `IQ4_XS` vs `Q4_K_M` vs `Q5_K_M` vs `Q6_K` vs `Q8_0` vs FP8/INT8/NVFP4 at fixed context/tasks under identical runtime; most evidence is model-card-specific.
- Public, reproducible BF16-reference KLD/PPL for the exact “basalt-ada-engine” target models on sm_89 was not found in accessible sources during this pass.

## What is imatrix (importance matrix) and how does it improve IQ4_XS quality?

### Takeaway
An imatrix is calibration-derived per-tensor (or per-element/channel aggregate) activation/importance statistics collected on representative text, used by `llama-quantize` to allocate quantization error toward less important weights and preserve salient ones; this is particularly valuable for i-quants/low-bit types such as `IQ4_XS`, often reducing perplexity/KLD degradation versus non-imatrix quantization. — [llama.cpp imatrix README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/tools/imatrix/README.md); [llama.cpp quantize README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/tools/quantize/README.md); [PR #4861 context referenced by imatrix README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/4861).

### Cited Findings
- `llama-imatrix` computes an importance matrix for a model and calibration dataset and is intended to enhance quantized model quality; workflow: run imatrix on calibration text, then `llama-quantize --imatrix imatrix.gguf ...`. — [llama.cpp imatrix README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/tools/imatrix/README.md)
- imatrix supports GGUF (default) and legacy formats, chunking/saving controls, merging inputs (`--in-file`), optional collection for output/token embedding (`--process-output`, default off), and can include MTP/NextN layers in supported setups. — [llama.cpp imatrix README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/tools/imatrix/README.md)
- imatrix statistics expose activation-energy and distribution properties (mean/stddev, skewness/kurtosis, entropy, ΣE[A²], inter-layer gain/L2/PCC/covariance), consistent with prioritizing high-energy/outlier-sensitive tensors during quantization. — [llama.cpp imatrix README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/tools/imatrix/README.md)
- `llama-quantize` accepts `--imatrix` and selective application via `--include-weights`/`--exclude-weights`; docs note imatrix “highly recommended” in quantization guidance/examples. — [llama.cpp quantize README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/tools/quantize/README.md)
- VisionPsy-Nano GGUF release states all sub-8-bit variants (except legacy Q4_0) use imatrix calibration, which “consistently reduces quality degradation”, and reports `IQ4_XS` (imatrix) materially better than legacy `Q4_0` on its benchmark suite. — [VisionPsy-Nano-460M-GGUFs](https://huggingface.co/qvac/VisionPsy-Nano-460M-GGUFs)

### Inferences
- For `IQ4_XS` on sm_89 (quality-first), imatrix should be treated as a default part of the recipe, with calibration data representative of target prompts/domains and with output/embedding handling reviewed (`--process-output` defaults/overrides, per-tensor exclusions) because quantization sensitivity is tensor-dependent.
- imatrix improves *allocation* of quantization error but cannot fully recover information lost at very low bpw; quality gains are empirical and must be measured (PPL/KLD/task deltas) per model.

### Gaps
- No universally recommended calibration corpus/size for imatrix was established from primary docs in this pass; calibration quality remains a practical tuning variable.
- Direct, isolated ablation numbers for `IQ4_XS` with vs without imatrix on the same large LLM and sm_89 runtime were not found beyond individual model cards.

## What is layer-specific quantization (attention vs FFN) and does it improve quality?

### Takeaway
Layer-/tensor-specific (mixed-precision) quantization assigns different bitwidths/types to different tensors (e.g. embeddings/output, attention projections, FFN/MoE experts) to preserve sensitive components while compressing tolerant ones; llama.cpp/ik_llama.cpp expose mechanisms for this, and practical evidence/model-card practice suggests it can improve quality-per-byte versus uniform low-bit quantization, though gains are model- and recipe-specific. — [llama.cpp quantize README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/tools/quantize/README.md); [ik_llama.cpp discussion #8](https://github.com/ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp/discussions/8); [VisionPsy-Nano-460M-GGUFs](https://huggingface.co/qvac/VisionPsy-Nano-460M-GGUFs).

### Cited Findings
- `llama-quantize` supports per-tensor overrides (`--tensor-type`, regex-capable, repeatable), keeping output tensor un(re)quantized (`--leave-output-tensor`), setting output and token embedding types (`--output-tensor-type`, `--token-embedding-type`), and include/exclude lists for imatrix application. — [llama.cpp quantize README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/tools/quantize/README.md)
- llama.cpp quantize examples show selective quantization patterns (e.g. setting `attn_v` and `ffn_down` to higher precision, or regex-based odd/even attention tensor assignments), demonstrating first-class support for heterogeneous tensor quantization recipes. — [llama.cpp quantize README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/tools/quantize/README.md)
- `llama-imatrix` defaults to not collecting/using importance data for `output.weight` and tied token embeddings (`--process-output` false), reflecting common practice that these tensors are sensitive and often better kept higher precision or handled specially. — [llama.cpp imatrix README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/tools/imatrix/README.md)
- ik_llama.cpp introduces/optimizes additional quant types (`IQ2_K..IQ6_K`, `IQ*_KS`, etc.) and emphasizes CPU/GPU kernel behavior differences by type/block layout; community usage often combines custom tensor-type recipes to fit quality into memory budgets (discussion context; implementation details vary by fork). — [ik_llama.cpp discussion: New quantization types IQ2_K, IQ3_K, IQ4_K, IQ5_K](https://github.com/ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp/discussions/8)
- VisionPsy-Nano GGUF card notes a mixed setup at the model level: language-model weights quantized per variant while vision encoder/projector (`mmproj`) is fixed to Q8_0 for all variants to protect multimodal quality. — [VisionPsy-Nano-460M-GGUFs](https://huggingface.co/qvac/VisionPsy-Nano-460M-GGUFs)

### Inferences
- “Layer-specific quantization” in GGUF practice usually means tensor-family-specific precision assignment (attn q/k/v/o, FFN up/gate/down, embeddings/lm_head, norms, MoE experts/router parts) rather than a single global dtype; this often improves BF16-proximity at a target size versus uniform 4-bit.
- For sm_89 quality-first dense/MoE engines, a reasonable starting policy is: keep embeddings/lm_head and known-sensitive tensors higher (BF16/F16/Q8_0/Q6_K), quantize bulk FFN/experts more aggressively, and validate with imatrix-informed mixed recipes.

### Gaps
- No definitive, general rule was found for “attention vs FFN: which always gets lower bits”; optimal splits are architecture- and task-dependent.
- The specific ResearchGate claim mentioned in local project notes (“IQ4_XS for attention, IQ2_XXS for FFN reduces error”) was not retrieved/verified from a primary source in this pass and should be treated as a lead, not evidence. — `~/<private>/projects/active/basalt-ada-engine/REVIEW_AND_STAGING.md`

## What are the dequantization parity requirements (CPU vs GPU, bit-exact, tolerance)?

### Takeaway
There is no universal “bit-exact across CPU and GPU” guarantee for all quantized inference paths; parity depends on quant format, kernel implementation, accumulation precision, and backend-specific optimizations. For quality-critical sm_89 engines, parity should be defined explicitly (bit-exact where feasible, otherwise numeric tolerances on logits/perplexity/KLD/task outputs) and validated per backend and per quantization recipe. — [llama.cpp quantize/imatrix docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/tools/quantize/README.md); [LMDeploy format/engine docs](https://github.com/InternLM/lmdeploy/blob/main/docs/en/quantization/w4a16.md); [llm-compressor convert/dequant tools](https://github.com/vllm-project/llm-compressor/blob/main/docs/guides/entrypoints/convert.md).

### Cited Findings
- llama.cpp separates concerns of weight storage/quantization (GGUF tensor types) and runtime kernels across backends; quantization tooling includes options that change tensor representations/mixes (e.g. requantize, pure, per-tensor types), implying that runtime behavior must be validated for the chosen configuration rather than assumed identical across environments. — [llama.cpp quantize README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/tools/quantize/README.md)
- HF/community evaluation practice for GGUF often uses `llama-perplexity --kl-divergence` (sometimes CPU-only for comparability) to compare quantized outputs to a reference, indicating practical reliance on measured divergence/PPL tolerances rather than formal bit-exact proofs. — [na0x2c6/Clover-35B-A3B-Preview-GGUF](https://huggingface.co/na0x2c6/Clover-35B-A3B-Preview-GGUF); [VisionPsy-Nano-460M-GGUFs](https://huggingface.co/qvac/VisionPsy-Nano-460M-GGUFs)
- LMDeploy supports multiple model formats/engines and precision-related settings (e.g. dtype/gemm input dtype/quant formats), which can change kernel selection and numerics; compatibility and quality are therefore configuration-dependent. — [LMDeploy quant/docs summaries via Context7](https://github.com/internlm/lmdeploy/blob/main/docs/en/api/pipeline.rst)
- llm-compressor provides conversion utilities such as `FP8BlockDequantizer` to reconstruct dense floating-point weights (default bfloat16) from FP8 block-quantized checkpoints for specific targets/block sizes, illustrating explicit dequantization pathways and dtype choices that affect parity with original dense behavior. — [llm-compressor convert docs](https://github.com/vllm-project/llm-compressor/blob/main/docs/guides/entrypoints/convert.md)

### Inferences
- For an sm_89 engine targeting BF16-quality, define parity tiers: (1) weight decode parity (dequantized tensor values vs reference within dtype/tolerance), (2) kernel/output parity (logits/top-k agreement on fixed prompts), and (3) end-task parity (KLD/PPL/benchmark deltas within thresholds). Bit-exact CPU==GPU is a strong goal but often unrealistic across fused kernels and different accumulators.
- Mixed quant recipes (per-tensor types, imatrix, different KV/cache dtypes) increase parity surface area; every recipe/backend pair needs regression tests.

### Gaps
- A formal, engine-agnostic tolerance standard for “BF16-equivalent” quantized inference on Ada was not found (e.g. required max logit error, KLD bound, or task-score delta thresholds).
- NInfer/`.ninfer` dequant semantics and CPU/GPU parity guarantees were not available from public sources in this pass.

## What artifact formats are relevant (.basalt, .ninfer, GGUF, safetensors) and their quality implications?

### Takeaway
`safetensors` is the common dense/reference weight container for BF16/FP16/FP8 checkpoints; `GGUF` is the dominant single-file quantized/runtime-oriented format in ggml/llama.cpp ecosystems (with rich tensor-type metadata and mmap-friendly layout goals); `.ninfer` and `.basalt` appear as project/engine-specific artifact formats in local basalt/NInfer workflows, but their public specs and quality guarantees are limited in accessible sources, making validation (conversion correctness, dequant parity, metadata completeness) critical. — [GGUF spec in ggml](https://github.com/ggml-org/ggml/blob/master/docs/gguf.md); [llama.cpp quantize README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/tools/quantize/README.md); `~/<private>/projects/active/basalt-ada-engine/project.md`.

### Cited Findings
- **GGUF**: GGML Universal File format with magic/version, KV metadata, tensor descriptors (name, dims, type, offset), alignment, and a tensor data blob; designed to carry model metadata and quantized tensor types in one artifact. — [ggml GGUF spec](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/ggml/master/docs/gguf.md); [ggml/llama.cpp headers and docs (Context7)](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/ggml/include/gguf.h)
- GGUF quantized artifacts can encode heterogeneous tensor precisions/types (K-quants, IQ quants, legacy quants, etc.), and llama.cpp supports conversion/quantization workflows into GGUF for deployment. — [llama.cpp quantize README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/tools/quantize/README.md); [VisionPsy-Nano-460M-GGUFs](https://huggingface.co/qvac/VisionPsy-Nano-460M-GGUFs)
- **safetensors** is widely used as source/reference format for HF models and as input/output for quantization pipelines (e.g. convert HF to GGUF, serve/quantize in LMDeploy/vLLM ecosystems) and is the natural carrier for BF16 reference weights. (Inference based on common toolchains cited above; direct safetensors spec not fetched in this pass.) — [llama.cpp quantize README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/tools/quantize/README.md); [LMDeploy docs](https://github.com/InternLM/lmdeploy/blob/main/docs/en/quantization/w4a16.md)
- **.ninfer**: local eval artifacts show NInfer serving models from `.ninfer` files (e.g. `qwen3_8_27b.ninfer`) with runtime flags for KV dtype/spec/prefill behavior, indicating an engine-specific packaged artifact; quality depends on conversion and runtime numerics but public format details were not retrieved. — `~/<private>/evals/2026-10-04/r14-ninfer-vs-strata/RESULTS.md`
- **.basalt**: local basalt-ada-engine project docs describe `.basalt` packs/converters as native artifacts (with inspect/probe/profiles) and identify an open choice between adopting `.basalt`, bridging `.ninfer`/GGUF, or using an internal IR; quality implications called out include encoding dense/MoE topology, expert layout/quant, KV/chat-template/vision, and validation via hash/inspect/dequant parity. — `~/<private>/projects/active/basalt-ada-engine/project.md`; `~/<private>/projects/active/basalt-ada-engine/RESEARCH_PREP.md`; `~/<private>/projects/active/basalt-ada-engine/REVIEW_AND_STAGING.md`

### Inferences
- For quality assurance on sm_89, `safetensors` (BF16) should remain the canonical reference; `GGUF` is the most inspectable/standardized quantized artifact path in open tooling; custom formats (`.ninfer`, `.basalt`) can be performant but require converter/probe/dequant parity tooling to be trusted for BF16-quality claims.
- Artifact metadata completeness (tokenizer/chat template/rope/vision/expert layout/KV assumptions) is part of “quality” in practice because mismatches can cause behavioral regressions unrelated to weight quantization.

### Gaps
- Public specifications for `.basalt` and `.ninfer` (tensor encoding, quant metadata, checksums, dequant reference behavior) were not found; only local references and naming were available.
- No comparative study was found on quality loss introduced by format conversion pipelines (safetensors→GGUF→engine-specific pack) under controlled settings.

## What papers/repos on HuggingFace or Chinese sources address quantization quality on Ada?

### Takeaway
The most relevant located paper for *quality/decision behavior* under low-bit quantization is “Which Decisions Low-Bit Quantization Breaks, and How to Predict Them” (arXiv:2608.06564), which studies decision flips/margin shrinkage across 4/3/2-bit settings (including GGUF/GPTQ contexts) rather than sm_89 kernel performance; VisionPsy-Nano (arXiv:2609.31746 and HF GGUF releases) provides a concrete example of IQ4_XS imatrix retention on a small VLM. Broad Chinese-engine ecosystem sources (e.g. LMDeploy, FastLLM repos/docs) discuss INT4/INT8/FP8 support including Ada (sm89) in places, but sm_89-specific, BF16-parity quantization-quality papers remain sparse in retrieved results. — [arXiv:2608.06564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06564); [arXiv:2609.31746](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31746); [VisionPsy-Nano-460M-GGUFs](https://huggingface.co/qvac/VisionPsy-Nano-460M-GGUFs); [LMDeploy W8A8](https://github.com/InternLM/lmdeploy/blob/main/docs/en/quantization/w8a8.md); [FastLLM](https://github.com/ztxz16/fastllm).

### Cited Findings
- **“Which Decisions Low-Bit Quantization Breaks, and How to Predict Them”** (Wu, Dhiman, Koshiyama; arXiv:2608.06564v5, 2026-08-06 submitted, revised 2026-10-05) studies 16 LMs from 8 families at 4/3/2 bits across PTQ settings (including GPTQ and GGUF), evaluating tool use, safety, knowledge, and social bias; finds an approximately linear pre/post decision-margin relationship `m' ≈ c m + b` with slope `c` usually <1 shrinking as precision falls, and reports predictable flip rates across 1,082 model/setting/bitwidth/decision-type combinations (median prediction error 1.0 pp vs 1.3 pp baseline). — [arXiv:2608.06564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06564)
- The same paper reports task-type sensitivity differences (e.g. in BFCL, “whether to call a tool” can be more sensitive than “which tool to call” under 3-bit RTN in some models) and notes fewer flips for some decisions under GPTQ/GGUF than plain rounding, implying quantization method matters beyond nominal bitwidth. — [arXiv:2608.06564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06564)
- **VisionPsy-Nano** (arXiv:2609.31746) presents a compact on-device VLM post-training recipe focused on accuracy/efficiency/reliability; while the abstract does not enumerate GGUF quant types, the official HF GGUF release provides explicit IQ4_XS (imatrix) quality retention numbers versus FP32 baseline and recommends imatrix sub-8-bit builds. — [arXiv:2609.31746](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31746); [VisionPsy-Nano-460M-GGUFs](https://huggingface.co/qvac/VisionPsy-Nano-460M-GGUFs)
- **LMDeploy** (InternLM ecosystem, widely used in Chinese/open-source serving contexts) documents AWQ/GPTQ INT4 and INT8/FP8 paths with Ada Lovelace (sm89, 40 series) listed among supported NVIDIA architectures for those quantization modes. — [LMDeploy W4A16](https://github.com/InternLM/lmdeploy/blob/main/docs/en/quantization/w4a16.md); [LMDeploy W8A8](https://github.com/InternLM/lmdeploy/blob/main/docs/en/quantization/w8a8.md)
- **FastLLM** (Chinese-origin project/repo) advertises broad INT4/INT8/FP8 inference support and deployment claims across GPU classes; accessible README emphasizes capability/performance more than controlled BF16-quality studies. — [FastLLM README](https://github.com/ztxz16/fastllm/blob/master/README.md)

### Inferences
- For sm_89 quality work, decision-sensitive evaluations (tool-use gating, safety refusals, structured-choice tasks) should complement PPL/KLD because 2608.06564 shows low-bit quantization can systematically alter margins and flip discrete decisions even when aggregate metrics look acceptable.
- HF model cards/repos remain the most practical source for concrete IQ4_XS/imatrix retention numbers today; paper-level evidence is improving but still not tightly coupled to specific Ada runtime kernels/artifacts.

### Gaps
- No retrieved paper provided a controlled, Ada/sm_89-only comparison of `IQ4_XS` vs `Q4_K_M/Q5_K_M/Q6_K/Q8_0/FP8` with identical model/runtime/task harness and BF16 reference.
- Chinese-language primary papers specifically targeting sm_89 quantization *quality parity* (beyond support statements/perf claims) were not identified in accessible sources during this pass; this is a likely search gap rather than proof of absence.

## Cross-cutting recommendations for an sm_89 BF16-quality quantization program

### Takeaway
A quality-first sm_89 quantization strategy should use BF16 (or agreed high-fidelity baseline) as reference, prefer standardized/inspectable artifacts where possible, adopt imatrix + mixed-precision tensor policies for 4–5 bit GGUF, and gate releases on multi-metric parity (KLD/PPL + decision/task evals + backend consistency), because low-bit effects are model-, tensor-, and task-dependent. — [arXiv:2608.06564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06564); [llama.cpp imatrix/quantize](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/tools/imatrix/README.md); [VisionPsy-Nano-460M-GGUFs](https://huggingface.co/qvac/VisionPsy-Nano-460M-GGUFs); `~/<private>/projects/active/basalt-ada-engine/project.md`.

### Cited Findings
- Quantization can systematically shrink decision margins and induce task-dependent flips at low bitwidths, so quality validation must include decision-sensitive tasks, not only perplexity. — [arXiv:2608.06564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06564)
- llama.cpp provides practical levers for quality: imatrix calibration, per-tensor quant types, output/embedding overrides, and `--pure` control; i-quant/K-quant families expose different bpw/quality/perf tradeoffs. — [llama.cpp quantize README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/tools/quantize/README.md); [llama.cpp imatrix README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/tools/imatrix/README.md)
- Empirical HF GGUF releases show imatrix-calibrated low-bit variants can substantially narrow quality gaps, but legacy/non-calibrated 4-bit baselines may lag significantly. — [VisionPsy-Nano-460M-GGUFs](https://huggingface.co/qvac/VisionPsy-Nano-460M-GGUFs)
- Local basalt/NInfer context indicates artifact/format choice (`.basalt` vs `.ninfer`/GGUF/bridge) should be evaluated on converter cost and validation capability (inspect/hash/dequant parity), which directly affects trust in quality claims. — `~/<private>/projects/active/basalt-ada-engine/project.md`; `~/<private>/projects/active/basalt-ada-engine/RESEARCH_PREP.md`

### Inferences
- Practical recipe for quality-first trials on sm_89: start from BF16 safetensors reference; build GGUF baselines (`Q8_0`, `Q6_K`, `Q5_K_M`, `Q4_K_M`, `IQ4_XS`+imatrix) with documented calibration corpus and tensor policy; compare against any `.ninfer`/`.basalt` artifacts using identical prompts/tasks and record KLD/PPL plus decision-flip-sensitive evals.
- Require per-artifact provenance and validation hooks (conversion logs, tensor-type map, hash/inspect, dequant spot checks) before treating custom formats as BF16-proxy evidence.

### Gaps
- This pass did not execute benchmarks on sm_89 hardware; all performance/quality observations are literature/docs/model-card/local-note based.
- A finalized, evidence-backed tensor sensitivity map for the target basalt-ada models (which layers/tensors to keep high precision) was not produced and requires model-specific experiments.
